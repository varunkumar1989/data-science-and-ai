A Realistic 12-Month Roadmap for a Beginner

Plan a year of learning in twelve steps, from Excel and SQL foundations through business intelligence, Python, statistics and machine learning, data engineering and cloud, GenAI and agents, to a portfolio and interview preparation. Each stage has weekly hours, a milestone, an assessment, and a plain statement of what employers generally expect.

● Gap-fill lesson✦ 2026Beginner⏱ 90 min
⬇ Download PDF
By the end of this lesson you can
  • Plan twelve months of learning with weekly hours and milestones
  • Choose the order of topics and explain why it matters
  • Set an assessment for each stage that proves the skill
  • Set realistic expectations about what employers look for

Why a roadmap, and why realistic

A roadmap turns a vague wish ("I want to work with data") into a sequence of skills, each proven by a piece of work. The timing matters: a beginner who plans ten hours a week and finishes in twelve months has a real chance; a plan of thirty hours a week for three months usually stalls by the fourth week.

Case studyTwo learners, one year

Two learners start in January with the same goal. The first studies whenever motivated and skips the projects; by December she has watched many videos and has nothing to show. The second follows a roadmap of about ten hours a week, finishes a project each stage and keeps a record of each milestone. By December he has three published projects and a plan for interviews. The difference is the proof, not the hours.

The twelve months

Months Focus Hours per week Milestone Assessment
1–2 Foundation: Excel, SQL basics, basic statistics, business analytics basics About 10 A cleaned dataset with a five-measure summary A timed SQL test and an Excel cleaning exercise
3–4 Business intelligence: Power BI, star schema, DAX, dashboard design About 10 A published dashboard with row-level security Dashboard review against a rubric
5–6 Programming and data: Python, pandas, visualisation, Git and GitHub About 12 A Python exploratory analysis of a public dataset in GitHub Code review with tests
7–8 Machine learning and statistics: supervised learning, evaluation, a churn model About 12 A churn model with a business recommendation Model evaluation and explanation
9–10 Data engineering and cloud: warehouse concepts, ETL and ELT, dbt, Airflow, one cloud About 12 A three-layer pipeline with tests Pipeline review and a failure drill
11 GenAI, agents and MCP: LLM APIs, RAG, agents, MCP About 12 A RAG assistant with citations and an evaluation Evaluation report
12 Portfolio and interviews: two published projects, CV, LinkedIn, mock interviews, readiness check About 15 Three mock interviews and two published projects Interview rubric

The hours are a guide. A week with fewer hours is fine if the milestone is still reached; it is better to extend a stage than to skip its assessment.

Why this order

  • Excel and SQL first because every later skill reads and writes tables. A learner who cannot join two tables cannot build a dashboard, a model or a pipeline.
  • Business intelligence before programming because the first useful output, a dashboard, needs no code and proves the learner can answer business questions.
  • Python before machine learning because models are built with code, and the data preparation takes most of the effort.
  • Statistics before machine learning because the evaluation of a model is statistics; without it, a high accuracy number means little.
  • Data engineering after analytics because pipelines exist to feed the analysis; the learner understands what the pipeline must deliver before building it.
  • GenAI and agents near the end because they depend on the data, retrieval and evaluation skills earlier, and they are where mistakes are most expensive without those skills.

::::tip Protect the assessments An assessment is the proof that the stage is complete. A learner who skips it has consumed the content without the skill. Book the assessment in the calendar before starting each stage. ::::

What each milestone proves

Milestone Skill it proves
Cleaned dataset with five measures Cleaning, checking and summarising data
Published dashboard with RLS Modelling, measures, sharing and access control
Python analysis in GitHub Code, version control and reproducible analysis
Churn model with recommendation Modelling, evaluation and communicating a decision
Three-layer pipeline with tests Engineering reliability and data quality
RAG assistant with evaluation Retrieval, grounding and measuring AI quality
Two published projects and mock interviews Ability to show work and explain it under questioning

What employers generally expect

Employers look for working projects with code and data, clear documentation, measured results, the ability to explain trade-offs, and a steady record of learning. A roadmap cannot promise a job or a salary, and this one does not. It can make a candidate's evidence clear and specific.

Watch outExpect the first role to be a stepping stone

Entry-level roles in analytics and data are competitive, and job titles vary between employers. A first role that uses part of the skill set is a good result, and the portfolio is what moves the next step.

Adjusting the plan

  • If a stage's assessment is failed, repeat the stage's milestone before moving on.
  • If your time drops, extend each stage rather than dropping the portfolio work.
  • If you already know one stage, take its assessment early and move on.
  • Review the roadmap at the end of each stage: what took longer, what helped, and what to change.
Interview cornerHow would you explain your learning plan to an interviewer?

Name the stages in order and the milestone each produced: a dashboard, a model, a pipeline, a RAG assistant. Explain why the order matters, show the two published projects, and say what you would learn next. Interviewers listen for a clear plan with evidence, not a long list of courses.

::::key What to remember - A realistic roadmap has weekly hours, a milestone and an assessment for each stage. - The order follows dependencies: data first, then analysis, programming, models, pipelines and AI. - Each milestone proves a specific skill; the portfolio makes the proof visible. - Employers look for evidence, clear documentation and explained trade-offs. - Extend a stage rather than skipping its assessment. ::::

Quick quiz5 questions · tap an answer to check it

1. Why is business intelligence placed before programming in the roadmap?

Answer: B. The early milestone builds motivation and evidence.

2. Why should statistics come before machine learning?

Answer: B. Evaluation depends on statistical reasoning.

3. What should a learner do if an assessment is failed?

Answer: B. The assessment proves the skill; repeat until it is shown.

4. Which milestone best proves interview readiness?

Answer: B. Published work and practised explanations are the evidence.

5. What does the roadmap promise?

Answer: B. The roadmap cannot promise employment.
ExerciseMake your own year

Adapt the roadmap to your situation: your weekly hours, your starting point, and the role you want. Write each stage with its months, hours, milestone and assessment. Then mark the two stages you would most likely skip under pressure and write how you will protect their assessments.

Show solution

Hints: start from the milestones, not the topics. If you have fewer hours, double the length of each stage rather than halving the content. Put the assessments in your calendar with dates.
